Craft Guide — Prevent Mold, Every Piece

Your own reference. Follow this in order on every piece, bark-on especially — this is what actually stops the mold problem, not the finish coat alone.

1. Dry it before you touch a finish

  • Green wood off your own property holds a lot of internal moisture — that's the root cause of mold, not bad luck.
  • Stand pieces somewhere dry and ventilated, off the ground, not stacked touching each other. Not direct sun (cracks it), not a closed damp space (defeats the point).
  • Bark-on pieces need longer — bark holds moisture far longer than the wood underneath it.
  • Rough guide: thin staffs/sticks (1–2" diameter) often need several weeks to a couple months with good airflow. Thicker pieces need longer. Don't guess — test it (next step).

2. Test moisture content — don't eyeball it

  • Get a pin-style wood moisture meter (~$20–30, one-time buy, pays for itself instantly).
  • Test before finishing every piece. The accepted mold threshold is 16–19% — stay under that, and aim closer to 8–12% for a piece that's genuinely stable and dry.
  • If it's still high, it needs more dry time — no shortcuts here, this is the step that actually prevents mold.

3. Clean the surface

  • Brush or wipe off loose bark, dirt, and any insect debris before treating.

4. Treat with a borate preservative

  • This is the actual mold/fungus/insect defense — apply it before any sealer, not instead of drying.
  • Options: a borax/water solution (cheap, DIY), or a commercial product like Tim-bor or Bora-Care (sold at hardware stores or online, easy to apply with a brush or spray).
  • Let it fully absorb and dry per the product's label before moving on.

5. Let it dry again after treatment

  • Don't rush straight to finishing — give the borate treatment time to fully dry first.

6. Apply the finish

  • For durability and moisture/mold resistance: spar urethane is the standard choice for outdoor wood pieces like walking sticks — built for UV and moisture exposure.
  • For a more natural look: a penetrating oil (Danish oil, tung oil) with a mildewcide additive mixed in — sold separately at most hardware/marine supply stores, a small amount goes a long way.
  • Either way, the borate step above is doing the actual mold-prevention work — the top coat is durability and looks.

7. Cure it right

  • Full cure time per the finish label, in a dry ventilated space — never sealed in plastic or a closed box right after finishing.
  • Only package/ship once it's fully cured.

Quick checklist per piece

  • ☐ Dried to target moisture (checked with meter, not guessed)
  • ☐ Surface cleaned
  • ☐ Borate-treated and re-dried
  • ☐ Finish applied
  • ☐ Fully cured before packaging
